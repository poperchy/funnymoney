Four out of five regressions came from a tag, not code

We traced every confirmed regression across 500 storefronts for 30 days. Most of them never went through a pull request, a code review, or a CI budget.

Every performance talk ends with the same advice: set a budget, fail the build. It’s good advice. It also assumes the thing making your site slower is in your repository.

For a month we watched a public sample of 500 storefronts with the same engine the product uses — one run per page per day, fixed conditions, every change confirmed by a second run. Then we asked a boring question of every confirmed regression: where did the bytes come from?

How we counted

A regression here means an event our feed would write: a resource that got heavier, appeared, or lost its caching, held across two runs, with a measurable cost in LCP, TBT or CLS. Filename hashes and rotating ad creatives were suppressed first — otherwise the numbers are meaningless.

We then attributed each event by the origin of the resource and, for first-party files, whether the change lined up with a deploy.

Tag-manager injections, chat and session-replay widgets, and ad or analytics pixels together account for 79% of events. First-party code that matched a deploy — the only category a CI budget can catch — is 16%.

The median site added its first new third-party script on day 6 — and nobody on the engineering side knew until day 17.

Why CI doesn’t see it

Lighthouse CI runs against a build. A GTM container is edited in a browser tab by someone in marketing, published on a Thursday afternoon, and live everywhere in seconds. There is no build. The budget never runs.

Even when it does, CI measures one page in a clean environment. The tag that fires only on /checkout after a consent banner is accepted doesn’t exist there.

What actually helps

Nothing here is new. The difference in our sample was whether teams did it continuously, after deploy, against production:

01Inventory third parties daily. A new domain on 34 pages is an event, whether or not it breaks a budget.
02Budget production, not builds. Same thresholds you’d put in CI, checked against what visitors actually load.
03Send it to whoever published the tag. The weekly digest in our sample was forwarded to marketing more often than to engineering.

The full anonymised sample, including per-site event logs, is on the benchmark page. It updates monthly.
